# Bailey's Bouncy World

Website Design Prompt: "The Dog with No Chill" — Bailey's World

Overall Goal

Design a website that feels like stepping inside the picture book itself — bright, bouncy, silly, and built for a child's eyes and attention span first, with a clean commerce/adoption layer underneath for the grown-ups. Every page should feel like Bailey is personally hosting the visitor.

1. Color Palette (pulled from existing brand graphics)

Role Color Notes Primary background Sky blue / cyan gradient Main canvas, feels like an open playground sky Headline text Deep navy blue High contrast, punchy, "shouting" energy Secondary accent Bright teal Subheadings, outlines, dashed borders Pop accent 1 Sunshine yellow Badges, stars, highlights Pop accent 2 Hot pink/magenta Fun callouts, buttons Pop accent 3 Grass green Ground elements, nature scenes, "go/active" cues Pop accent 4 Orange Secondary stars/sparkles, energy bursts Ground/base Grass green + brown (tree trunks, dirt paths) Keeps scenes feeling like an outdoor park

Use color the way the source graphics do: big flat blocks of bright color, white outlines around text and characters so everything pops off the background, and no muddy or muted tones — kids' eyes want saturation.

2. Typography

Headlines: Chunky, rounded, hand-lettered "bubble" font with a slight wobble — should look like it was drawn by hand, not typed. Words can tilt, stack, or bounce along a curved baseline for playfulness.

Body text: Simple, rounded sans-serif, generously sized (kids and parents skimming on phones) — never small, never a thin/serif font.

Reading level: Short sentences, simple words, lots of exclamation points, direct address ("Hi, I'm Bailey!"). Avoid corporate language anywhere on the child-facing pages.

3. Character & Illustration Style

Bailey (the fluffy white dog) should appear on nearly every page as a guide/host — waving, running, pointing at buttons, reacting with expressions (surprised, excited, sleepy).

Hand-drawn, comic-style linework: thick black outlines, exaggerated bouncy poses, motion lines, little sweat-drops or sparkle-bursts for energy.

Recurring graphic motifs to reuse across the site: cloud-shaped speech bubbles, dashed-outline quote bubbles ("Bailey Says..."), solid and outline stars, confetti squiggles, paw prints as bullet points/dividers.

Every section should have a small Bailey illustration doing something relevant (Bailey with a shopping bag on the shop page, Bailey with a heart on the adoption page, etc.) so kids always feel like they're being led through the site by a character, not a menu.

4. Making It Genuinely Engaging for Kids

This is the part that turns a "pretty" site into a fun one:

Big, tappable buttons shaped like stickers or badges (rounded, thick outline, drop shadow) — sized for small fingers on tablets/phones, not tiny text links.

Hover/tap micro-animations: Bailey wags her tail, stars twinkle, buttons bounce or squish slightly when pressed — small delightful feedback everywhere.

Sound-optional character voice: an optional "Bailey barks hello!" sound on the homepage (muted by default, toggle on) for younger visitors.

A mascot-guided navigation: instead of a plain text menu, use icon + Bailey-illustrated buttons (a book icon for "Meet the Book," a bone/paw for "Shop," a heart for "Adopt," a farm barn for "Shelter Farm").

Mini interactive moments: a simple "find the tennis ball" hidden-object game or a coloring-page download/printable activity tied to the book's characters — gives kids a reason to linger, not just scroll.

Progress/reward badges: small collectible star badges kids "earn" for exploring different sections (purely front-end/localStorage-free, just for delight, not needed to unlock anything).

Read-along preview: an embedded flipbook-style preview of a few pages from the book with big illustrations, so kids get a taste of the story before asking a parent to buy it.

Friendly loading/empty states: instead of blank screens, show Bailey "fetching" something with a fun animation while content loads.

5. Site Sections

Home / Hero: Big Bailey illustration mid-action, bold tagline in a speech-bubble, "Meet Bailey" CTA button styled as a sticker badge.

Meet the Book(s): Cover art, flip-preview, "Bailey Says" quote callouts pulled straight from the books, release date countdown for the November title.

Shop / Merchandise: Same playful visual language, but interaction should tighten up here — clear product cards, big "Add to Cart" badge-buttons, a smooth, trustworthy, well-tested checkout/payment flow (this is where the fun styling meets functional clarity — kids browse, parents pay).

Adoption Program (UK): Warm, heart-forward section for organizations submitting dogs for adoption — Bailey with a heart icon, simple explainer in 2–3 short steps, clear submission CTA.

Shelter Farm: Its own mini-world within the site — photos/illustrations of the farm, a "Bailey visits the farm" storytelling section.

Activities/Downloads: Printable coloring pages, simple games, wallpaper downloads — the "fun zone" that keeps kids coming back.

Parents' Corner (optional footer section): Where the grown-up info lives — shipping, returns, contact, socials — styled more simply so it doesn't compete visually with the kid-facing content.

6. Tone Summary

Warm, silly, energetic, and treat the visitor (child or parent) like they're being welcomed into Bailey's world, not landing on a store. Every element should ask: "Would a 6-year-old want to tap this, giggle at this, or show this to a friend?" If not, make it bigger, bouncier, or more Bailey.
